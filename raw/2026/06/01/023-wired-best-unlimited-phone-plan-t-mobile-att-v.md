Unlimited cell phone plans often come with a long list of footnotes, terms, conditions, and exceptions. Mercifully, all of the big three cell companies—Verizon, AT&amp;T, and T-Mobile—have, by now, ditched throttling on their most expensive plans and include 5G data access in all of their unlimited plans. Yet there are still many differences in the services they offer, including differences between each carrier&#x27;s various tiers. Cheaper “unlimited” tiers do offer unlimited talk and text. But they&#x27;re not universally unlimited in that they still have rules on how much data you get before the carriers start throttling your speed. Some supposed unlimited plans may throttle your data at any given time. It&#x27;s been a fixture of cell service plans for years. These details can be overwhelming to sort when trying to pick the best unlimited cell phone plan for your needs. Below, I&#x27;ve highlighted what each of the three major carriers offers for “unlimited” individual and family plans to help you figure out which option is best for you and your budget. If you want to avoid slower data speeds at peak times, you&#x27;ll likely be choosing among the plans here. The carrier also makes claims to winning on value, taking into account perks like entertainment bundles, airplane Wi-Fi, and access to satellite data in emergencies. T-Mobile has rebranded its unlimited offerings this year but still offers three (well, kind of four—I&#x27;ll explain below) main unlimited talk and text plans: Essentials, Experience More, and Experience Beyond. Only the two Experience plans offer true unlimited 5G data without any throttling or deprioritizing (i.e., making your phone stand in line for data behind other, more important phones during peak demand). For occasional jet-setters and those who want consistent phone upgrades, Experience More is the best affordable phone plan among all services. It includes Wi-Fi during flights, 4K video, 60 gigs of high-speed mobile hot-spotting, a modicum of international data, a one-year AAA membership, free ad-supported Netflix, and cheap ($3) Apple TV. It&#x27;ll also let you upgrade your phone every two years so random children don&#x27;t make fun of your ancient iPhone at a rest stop. (Yes, this has happened to me.) If you want three lines or more and you&#x27;re a new T-Mobile customer (or a five-year T-Mobile customer), you have access to an even better deal. The limited-time-only Better Value plan is in fact, possibly the best deal in phonedom at the moment. For the same price as the three-to-five-line Experience More plans, you add free ad-supported Hulu, access to the T-Satellite emergency network, unlimited mobile hot-spotting, 30 gigs of international data in hundreds of countries, plus additional watch and tablet lines for just $5 a line. With the exception of phone upgrades every two years instead of every year, most perks are better than T-Mobile&#x27;s highest-price plan, at a much lower price. This sale price is also locked in for 5 years. If cost is more important to you than perks, you have a big family, and you don&#x27;t travel a lot internationally, the Essentials plan is probably best. It&#x27;s no frills, just the phone, ma&#x27;am, with no subscription money going to streaming services or international carrier fees. Your hot spot is limited to 3G speeds, which is an important consideration for many laptop warriors, and video streaming is 480p. But at its price point, Essentials is the only plan among the big three carriers to offer premium data (up to 50 GB) that won&#x27;t be throttled. If you&#x27;ve got home Wi-Fi, that 50GB should be sufficient for most people. Besides, it&#x27;ll teach your teens how to budget and how to ask for the Wi-Fi password instead of using their own data. But take note: Data in Canada and Mexico will be so slow, you might as well have an old flip phone. Note that the Essentials Saver plan—an under-advertised super-discount plan for many years—is basically identical to T-Mobile&#x27;s Essentials plan in terms of data speeds and service. But it&#x27;s $10 less and does not offer access to any phone promo discounts, phone financing, or multi-line discounts. It&#x27;s also good for only up to two lines. But if you&#x27;re single and already have a phone, it&#x27;s the cheapest plan on offer, and you still get 50 gigs of utterly unsquelched data. Just make sure to get your own cheap phone . Do you actually need Experience Beyond , T-Mobile&#x27;s most premium plan? Maybe not, unless you are a true digital nomad or a nomad in general—or just hate the idea of limitation. If this describes you, you&#x27;ll like that the Beyond plan offers unlimited high-speed mobile hot-spotting, doubles or triples your international data, and lets you upgrade your phone every year so that no one ever has anything you don&#x27;t. It also adds ad-supported Hulu (but not Disney). Most essential for backcountry trekkers, Beyond adds access to T-Satellite data, which is to say Starlink data . So if you happen to get mauled by a bear miles from the nearest cell tower, you can still share your location, text 911, and send SMS messages and photos. This service can be added to other plans for a $10 premium. WIRED: 5G data speeds are available on every tier, as is international texting and unlimited talk, text, and data in Mexico and Canada (possibly nonfunctional 2G data speeds on Essentials, 30 GB of 5G data on Experience and Better Value plans). First responders , military and veteran families , and those aged 55-plus all get discounts. (For those 55 and up, T-Mobile is by far the most economical.) The Essentials plan will not throttle back your data speeds until you&#x27;ve used 50 GB, which is still a lot of data.
