# The Token Do-or-Die Line: Financial AI Companies Scramble to Cut Costs

**来源**: 钛媒体 | **发布**: 2026-05-20 | **链接**: https://www.tmtpost.com/7996225.html

## RSS 摘要

Fine-grained control over token costs is shifting from an optional exercise for enterprises to a must-have requirement.

## 正文

NextFin News -- "To be frank, the window of opportunity isn&rsquo;t long anymore. For companies, an AI transformation may have to be completed within the next two years," said&nbsp;Liang Zhongzhi, Senior Technical Director at Yinmi Fund. He noted that companies that complete the transformation first will gain enormous cost and efficiency advantages, enabling them to lock in incremental growth in their respective markets. In his view, AI transformation is no longer about development&mdash;it&rsquo;s a matter of corporate survival. However, most companies&rsquo; AI implementation efforts are currently stuck. Many vendors and apps are simply &ldquo;stuffing&rdquo; an AI assistant into their existing interaction model, yet still fail to truly solve users&rsquo; business problems, resulting in generally low usage intensity. The core issue often isn&rsquo;t the technology itself, but the fact that existing production relationships can&rsquo;t align with the new productive forces&mdash;and reshaping those relationships is an extremely painful process. According to McKinsey&rsquo;s The State of Organizations 2026 , as many as 88% of AI pilot projects failed to scale. The main reasons were not technical flaws, but rather the absence of evaluation mechanisms and governance barriers. Insufficient organizational readiness, as a &ldquo;slow variable,&rdquo; is more concealed than technological risk. Yinmi Fund&rsquo;s exploration is highly instructive. Starting in 2026, the company proactively pursued change and launched an AI-driven overhaul. On the R&amp;D side alone, all roles were consolidated into a single role: &ldquo;product engineer.&rdquo; Its AI assistant, &ldquo;AI Xiaogu,&rdquo; has cumulatively handled more than 1 million user questions. When token consumption reaches real-world usage at the level of a million tokens per day, cost is no longer an abstract figure&mdash;it becomes a very real bill. According to a recent public disclosure by Yinmi Fund Chairwoman Xiao Wen, Yinmi has deployed more than 200 models internally, with monthly token consumption reaching the hundreds-of-billions level. AI is no longer an experimental project; it has truly become as fundamental as water, electricity, and gas&mdash;an everyday necessity for ordinary employees in their daily work. Three Token-related Issues in Financial Scenarios Before exploring cost-reduction paths, Yinmi Fund tried a range of approaches, including tiered model scheduling, prompt streamlining, caching and precomputation, and RAG optimization. These delivered results, but the team hoped to find a solution closer to the underlying logic. Liang Zhongzhi&rsquo;s analysis suggests that token usage in financial scenarios has three major characteristics that directly drive up costs: First, the context is exceptionally long. Financial decision-making requires synthesizing a large amount of information&mdash;one client&rsquo;s holdings data, trading history, risk preferences, and communication records. Put together, that can easily run into several thousand or even tens of thousands of tokens. That&rsquo;s simply not in the same ballpark as writing a piece of code completion. Second, the accuracy bar is extremely high. Individual users might tolerate an AI-written blog post being a bit wordy, but businesses can&rsquo;t tolerate AI getting the return calculation wrong in an investment recommendation. This means financial scenarios often require stronger (and therefore more expensive) models, as well as more inference steps. Third, the &ldquo;value density&rdquo; varies enormously from one scenario to another. A user asking &ldquo;What is fund dollar-cost averaging?&rdquo; and a high-net-worth client asking &ldquo;How should I allocate my 5 million in assets?&rdquo; may consume roughly the same number of tokens, but the business value differs by orders of magnitude. &ldquo;The term &lsquo;token anxiety&rsquo; is spot-on,&rdquo; but in Liang Zhongzhi&rsquo;s view, it is more a product of a cognitive stage: the anxiety often comes from &ldquo;not knowing whether it&rsquo;s worth it.&rdquo; If you can clearly calculate the business value corresponding to every unit of token consumption, the anxiety will disappear. Beyond common forms of waste such as &ldquo;showboating calls,&rdquo; &ldquo;brute-force context stuffing,&rdquo; and &ldquo;duplicate reasoning,&rdquo; Liang Zhongzhi highlighted an even more hidden kind of waste: &ldquo;using probabilistic reasoning to solve deterministic problems.&rdquo; These are scenarios that should have been built as traditional software&mdash;build once, reuse indefinitely&mdash;but instead are repeatedly handed off to AI, creating linear costs out of thin air. Taken together, this waste may account for more than 50% of an enterprise&rsquo;s token consumption in AI applications. To address this, Yingmi Fund developed a &ldquo;token arbitrage&rdquo; framework: Step one: determine whether the scenario has an optimal solution. If it does, the best approach is to develop it as traditional software&mdash;build once, reuse indefinitely, with zero marginal cost &mdash;such as a fund screener, NAV lookup, or account overview. Step two: if you determine there&rsquo;s no optimal solution, then look at whether Token arbitrage holds. In a linear-cost setting, Token consumption is essentially paying for &ldquo;leverage that grows nonlinearly.&rdquo; Based on this, YMI Fund chose to invest heavily in Tokens for financial advisory scenarios&mdash;so that each Token replaces not a few cents of compute cost, but tens or even hundreds of yuan in marginal labor cost. &ldquo;Machines in the Industrial Revolution were a one-time investment with marginal costs trending toward zero; machines in the AI era are pay-per-use, so marginal costs don&rsquo;t go to zero. In the era of traditional software, you aimed for build once, reuse indefinitely; in the AI era, what you&rsquo;re aiming for is that every single call creates positive value. That&rsquo;s a fundamental shift in mindset. &rdquo; Liang Zhongzhi pointed out. Make Tokens Something Other Than a Cost Center In fact, fine-grained control of token costs is shifting from an elective to a required course for enterprises. A Goldman Sachs report, released in May 2026, noted that the AI industry is moving from a cost narrative to a profit narrative. The report showed that token pricing for mainstream large models, which had previously been falling by about 40% a year, has begun to stabilize, while the compute cost per token&mdash;driven by NVIDIA, AMD, Google TPU, and others&mdash;has continued to drop at an annual rate of 60%&ndash;70%. The &ldquo;scissor gap&rdquo; between the two curves is opening up profit headroom. Goldman Sachs projected that by 2030, consumer- and enterprise-side Agents combined will drive global Token consumption to 24 times the 2026 level, reaching roughly 120 quadrillion Tokens per month. &ldquo;If modern Chinese uses fewer tokens than English, then what about classical Chinese&mdash;one of the highest&ndash;information-density written forms among human languages? Could that work too?&rdquo; In late 2024, a wave of &ldquo;learn Chinese to save tokens&rdquo; went viral on overseas social media: U.S. developers found that expressing the same meaning in Chinese used far fewer Tokens than in English. Liang Zhongzhi verified this with hands-on tests: he wrote the same passage in English, modern Chinese, and Classical Chinese, then calculated Token usage. The result was striking&mdash;Classical Chinese used only about 30%&ndash;40% as many Tokens as English.
