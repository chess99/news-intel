Julie Qiu explains how AI serves as a "thinking partner" for engineering leaders. She discusses five distinct roles - Archaeologist, Experimenter, Critic, Author, and Reviewer - to manage the cognitive load of 400+ repositories. The Five Distinct Roles of AI Today I want to talk a little bit about how AI has helped me reason about the system, and how it&#39;s also helping me redesign it for the future. Usually when we talk about AI in software, what we talk about is developer productivity. We talk about moving faster. We talk about code assistants. We talk about autocomplete. In my role of TL in an organization of over 70 people, the bottleneck for me hasn&#39;t been with typing. My bottleneck has actually been around holding context. I don&#39;t really need something to write code for me, but what I do need is more RAM. I need a way to hold the state of everything that&#39;s happening across our org and across over 400 repositories, and for all the relevant pieces and all the information I want to just be surfaced to me at the time that I need it. I needed a way to not have to hold it all in my head at the same time. Over the past years, I&#39;ve been using AI to help me with this problem, I&#39;ve noticed that five distinct roles have emerged for me. The first is AI as an archaeologist. I use AI to help me look through the system and piece together what&#39;s really going on. The second has been AI as an experimenter. What AI lets me do is it&#39;ll let me simulate the ideas that I have and then be able to figure out whether or not that idea will hold up before I commit engineers to months of work. The third is AI as a critic. I&#39;ll often talk to AI and say, tell me what&#39;s wrong with my design, and it&#39;ll happily tell me what&#39;s going on. The fourth is AI as a co-author. I actually use AI to help me write production-quality code. On the flip side of that is using AI as a code reviewer. It helps me elevate the quality of my code by catching issues and clarifying logic before I have to send it off to a human reviewer.
